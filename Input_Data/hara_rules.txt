RULE SEV-1 (Severity S1)
Class S1 means light or moderate injuries are expected for the hazardous event.

RULE SEV-2 (Severity S2)
Class S2 means severe injuries are expected, but survival of the persons involved is probable.

RULE SEV-3 (Severity S3)
Class S3 means life-threatening injuries are possible (survival uncertain) or fatal injuries are expected.

RULE EXP-1 (Exposure E1)
Class E1 means the operational situation is very rare. It occurs only in unusual circumstances.

RULE EXP-2 (Exposure E2)
Class E2 means the operational situation is rare. It occurs a few times in the life of a vehicle.

RULE EXP-3 (Exposure E3)
Class E3 means the operational situation occurs sometimes. It is met regularly but not on every trip.

RULE EXP-4 (Exposure E4)
Class E4 means the operational situation is frequent. It occurs on almost every trip or for a large share of driving time.

RULE CTL-1 (Controllability C1)
Class C1 means almost all drivers can easily keep control and avoid harm after the malfunction.

RULE CTL-2 (Controllability C2)
Class C2 means most drivers can keep control and avoid harm, but some may not.

RULE CTL-3 (Controllability C3)
Class C3 means most drivers cannot keep control, or control is very difficult after the malfunction.

RULE ASIL-1 (ASIL determination)
The ASIL is determined only by a deterministic rule, never by the language model. If S, E or C is 0 the result is QM. Otherwise add the class numbers S+E+C. A sum of 10 gives ASIL D, 9 gives ASIL C, 8 gives ASIL B, 7 gives ASIL A, and 6 or less gives QM.

RULE ASIL-2 (QM events)
A hazardous event rated QM needs no safety goal. It is still recorded in the HARA worksheet so the decision stays traceable.

RULE HAZ-1 (Guideword method)
Hazard identification takes each item function and applies guidewords such as no or loss, unintended, too much, too little, too late, wrong direction, and stuck or intermittent. Each result is a malfunctioning behaviour.

RULE HAZ-2 (Hazardous event)
A hazardous event is a malfunctioning behaviour combined with an operational situation. Each hazardous event gets its own S, E and C rating with a written rationale.

RULE SG-1 (Safety goal wording)
A safety goal is written as: Avoid the hazard caused by the malfunction. It states the ASIL, the safe state and the fault tolerant time interval (FTTI).

RULE SG-2 (Safety goal coverage)
Every approved hazardous event with ASIL A, B, C or D needs at least one safety goal. The safety goal takes the ASIL of its hazardous event.

RULE REQ-1 (Atomic wording)
A safety requirement is one sentence that uses the word shall exactly once.

RULE REQ-2 (No vague words)
A safety requirement avoids vague words such as appropriate, adequate, fast, sufficient, reasonable, and/or, etc.

RULE REQ-3 (Measurable and ASIL)
A safety requirement states a measurable value with a unit, for example a time in ms or a torque in Nm, and carries the ASIL of its parent.

RULE REQ-4 (Parent and allocation)
A functional safety requirement (FSR) refers to its parent safety goal and is allocated to an architecture element. A technical safety requirement (TSR) refers to its parent FSR.

RULE REQ-5 (Verification)
Every safety requirement names a verification method, for example HIL fault injection, unit test or review.

RULE TRC-1 (Traceability completeness)
Traceability is complete when every approved hazardous event with an ASIL has a safety goal, every safety goal has an FSR, every FSR has a TSR, every requirement has a verification method, and ASIL values are inherited without change.

RULE GOV-1 (Human approval)
All AI output is a draft. A qualified engineer must approve or reject every hazardous event, safety goal and requirement. The system never approves automatically.
